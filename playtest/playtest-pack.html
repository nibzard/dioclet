The game in one minute

305 AD. Four rivals prepare a claim to the throne. Collect equipment on the palace map, earn Prestige, then turn the board over: on the succession map, buy the backing of two factions and proclaim at the Peristyle. A claim that survives 2 full rounds wins.

Set the table

Choose four humans or bots. Random gates use the game seed. You can also choose four distinct gates manually, or rotate gates across four games. Names and colors belong to players, not gates. The displayed assignment is final when you start.

Both maps share one street plan. The palace map comes first; the succession map is the same city with its Peristyle three steps east, 4 guard posts of its own, and six face-down faction cards. Every gate on the succession map is nine steps from its Peristyle.

Your turn

  1. Roll two six-sided dice for movement.
  2. Move along the circles, up to the total. You may stop early and pass other pawns.
  3. Do one action at your destination: take equipment, stand guard, duel, search a faction card, gain support, claim the checkpoint, proclaim, maintain your claim, or reveal a district with the galea.

Tap a highlighted circle. Collection is automatic when no competing action exists. Otherwise a modal shows the choices. A guard action or duel requires movement of at least one space. The other actions can be stationary.

Equipment stays yours

Six treasure entries hold six shuffled equipment types. Cards start face down; two begin face up. Each player can collect each type once, and only while the palace phase lasts. Equipment is never lost.

ItemAbility
Gladius · sword+1 to your attack in any duel you start
Scutum · shield+1 to your defense in any duel
Pilum · javelin+1 to your attack against the standing claimant
Pugio · daggerYou win tied duels if only you hold it. Both or neither: the attacker wins. Always active.
Galea · helmetOnce per game, as your action: reveal any one face-down treasure card
Lorica segmentata · armorOnce per game: when your claim is beaten, keep your square

In a duel each side adds at most one number bonus - the best one that applies, never a sum. The pugio is not a number bonus: it is a passive tie-break that works next to your chosen bonus whenever the final totals are equal. Gladius and pugio together are legal; gladius and pilum together still add only +1.

Earn and spend Prestige

You can hold at most 3 Prestige. Extra rewards are lost. Move to a guard post (either map) and roll one die: 4 or higher earns one Prestige. The winner of a duel earns one Prestige. The loser pays one back to the bank.

Resolve a duel in this order: both roll one die; each side adds its one best number bonus; compare totals; only if the totals are equal, apply the pugio - the lone holder wins, otherwise the attacker wins. Nobody loses equipment or support.

Search the faction cards

At the change to the succession map, six faction cards go face down on the ? spaces: the Army, the Imperial officials, the City elites, and three empty streets. End your move on a ? and turn its card face up: that is a search. Everyone sees it; it stays face up.

Stand on a face-up faction card and spend 1 Prestige as your action for its support. An empty street gives nothing. Every faction can support every player once. You need 2 different supporters to proclaim. Faction cards exist only in the succession phase.

From the palace to the succession

The first player who owns all six items may stand at the Peristyle and claim the checkpoint as their action. Finish that round, then 1 additional full round, and the succession begins. If nobody claims by round 24, the succession begins anyway.

At the change the palace map leaves the table. Every pawn returns to its gate on the succession map and keeps everything it owns: items, Prestige, and support already gained. Nothing is granted for free.

Proclaim and contest a claim

  1. During the succession phase, stand at the Peristyle with 2 supporters and proclaim. Only one claim can stand at a time.
  2. Your claim now stands inside a 2-round window. Rivals cancel it only by winning a challenge duel.
  3. On your turn you may maintain the claim and stay put. Leaving the Peristyle by your own move cancels it.
  4. If no rival cancels the claim before 2 full rounds pass, you are emperor.

To challenge, end your move within two street circles of the claimant and duel them. Challenging a standing claim costs a mandatory 1 Prestige fee, paid before you roll - even if you lose. Without at least 1 Prestige you cannot contest at all. A winning challenge cancels the claim; the claimant returns to their gate unless the lorica segmentata is unused, which once per game lets a beaten claimant keep the square (the claim still dies). If no claim survives by round 24 of the succession, the game ends in a draw.

What you can see

Positions, equipment, Prestige, supporters, revealed cards, and claims are public. Face-down treasure and faction cards and future rolls are hidden. The same seed and setup reproduce the game.

History behind the game

Diocletian's Tetrarchy shared rule between two senior emperors and two junior emperors. This game imagines rival claims after his retirement. Its three support groups and palace contest are game abstractions.

The gates have real names: Golden north, Silver east, Bronze south, and Iron west. The cardo runs north–south and the decumanus east–west. The six-block game map is not an exact reconstruction.

Imperial Rivals: 305 AD, rules V0.10.2. Simulations can test incentives and strategies; only human playtests can establish whether these choices are clear and enjoyable.

Teach in five minutes

  1. Show the six equipment locations and the lasting equipment bonus.
  2. Show where Prestige comes from and how one support action works.
  3. Walk through a turn with two movement dice and one action.
  4. Show the Peristyle and demonstrate a claim, a challenge, and the three responses.
  5. Explain that rivals keep their equipment and support after a canceled claim.

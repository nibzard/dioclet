The game in one minute

Four rivals prepare a claim to imperial power. Collect equipment, earn Prestige, and secure faction support. During succession, reach the Peristyle with 2 different supporters and proclaim your claim. If it survives a response turn from every rival, you win.

Set the table

Choose four humans or bots. Random gates use the game seed. You can also choose four distinct gates manually, or rotate gates across four games. Names and colors belong to players, not gates. The displayed assignment is final when you start.

The six intact blocks hold six shuffled equipment types. Cards start face down. Each player can collect each type once. The north and south gates end the cardo; the east and west gates end the decumanus. The cardo is off-center in this game map.

Your turn

  1. Roll two six-sided dice for movement.
  2. Move along the circles, up to the total. You may stop early and pass other pawns.
  3. Do one action at your destination. Taking equipment, gaining support, and fighting are separate actions.

Tap a highlighted circle. Collection is automatic when no competing action exists. Otherwise a modal shows the choices. A guard action or duel requires movement of at least one space. Equipment, support, a proclamation, and maintaining a claim can be stationary.

Equipment stays yours

Visit a treasure entry to collect its equipment. The first collection reveals the card to everyone. You can still collect equipment during succession. Equipment is never lost or given to you for free at transition.

Earn and spend Prestige

You can hold at most 3 Prestige. Extra rewards are lost. Move to a guard post and roll one die: 4, 5, or 6 earns one Prestige. The winner of a duel earns one Prestige. The first complete collector who opens the checkpoint also earns one.

To duel, end your move within two street spaces of a rival. Each player rolls one die and adds the equipment bonus. The attacker wins ties. Before rolling, the attacker may spend 1 Prestige for +2. The cost is paid even if the challenge fails. No one loses equipment or support.

ArmyDistrict F · barracks

City elitesDistrict A · civic meeting place

In both phases, visit a meeting place and spend 1 Prestige for its support. There is no additional roll. Every group can support every player, once per player. Support remains yours for the whole game. You need 2 different supporters to proclaim a claim. Rivals do not need to endorse you.

From preparation to succession

The first player with all six equipment types can open the checkpoint. Finish that round and 1 additional full round. Then succession begins. If no one opens it, succession starts after 24 city rounds.

Keep every pawn at its current position. Keep your equipment, Prestige, and support. Missing equipment is not granted. You can finish collecting it through play.

Proclaim and contest a claim

  1. During succession, reach the Peristyle with 2 supporters. Use your action to proclaim your claim.
  2. Every rival gets a response turn. They can challenge you, prepare their own position, or take another action.
  3. A winning challenge cancels your claim and returns your pawn to your assigned gate. Your equipment and support remain.
  4. If all three rivals finish a response with your claim intact, you are acclaimed emperor.

Only one claim can be pending. A failed challenge leaves it active. Leaving the Peristyle cancels your own claim. If your turn comes during the response window, you can maintain your claim and stay put. If nobody wins within 24 succession rounds, the game is a draw.

Challenging an active claimant costs 1 Prestige, paid before you roll. This fee is mandatory and separate from the optional +2 boost. You pay it even if the challenge fails. An ordinary duel against a player without an active claim stays free. A winning challenge still earns the normal duel reward.

What you can see

Positions, equipment, Prestige, supporters, and claim responses are public. Unrevealed equipment and future rolls remain hidden. The same seed and setup reproduce the game. The game does not use real money.

History behind the game

Diocletian's Tetrarchy shared rule between two senior emperors and two junior emperors. This game imagines rival claims after his retirement. Its three support groups and palace contest are game abstractions.

The gates have real names: Golden north, Silver east, Bronze south, and Iron west. The cardo runs north–south and the decumanus east–west. The six-block game map is not an exact reconstruction.

Succession prototype V0.8. The starting economy is experimental. Simulations can test incentives and strategies; only human playtests can establish whether these choices are clear and enjoyable.

Teach in five minutes

  1. Show the six equipment locations and the lasting equipment bonus.
  2. Show where Prestige comes from and how one support action works.
  3. Walk through a turn with two movement dice and one action.
  4. Show the Peristyle and demonstrate a claim, a challenge, and the three responses.
  5. Explain that rivals keep their equipment and support after a canceled claim.

# Human playtest protocol (V0.9.2 crossflow)

Status: prepared. Builds on `protocol-v08.md` and on everything
session 001 (`human-session-001.md`) changed at the table. This file
tests the V0.9.4 candidate: six distinct equipment abilities, two
districts revealed at setup, the crossflow palace board (two
interior treasures, four interior guard posts), and a separate
succession map for the endgame - everyone returns to their gate on
the new board, buys faction support at three meeting places (one
per street arm), and fights the claim window there with no guard
posts (duel wins are the only Prestige income in the endgame).

Record sessions in `human-session-00X.md` as observations only.
Keep predictions in a separate notes column.

## Materials

One printed 0.9.4 A4 kit from `print/` (tiled palace board, the
one-page succession map, player mats, six ability cards, rulebook,
quick reference, teaching guide, feedback form), two six-sided
movement dice, one six-sided duel die, counters for Prestige, four
pawns, a timer, and a pencil.

Record with each session: the date, `rules_v094_twinboard.json` and
its hash, the seed and gate assignment used, and the number of
rounds per board.

## Recommended groups

Same groups as V0.8. The priority is a second session with the
session-001 players: they redesigned the claim system themselves, so
their reaction to the window and shame is the sharpest test.

Play at least one four-game gate rotation with the adult strategy
group. Each player uses every gate once. The east gate is the
measured weak seat (about 16% wins in simulation against 27-29% for
the others): note whether anyone feels it before they are told.

## Facilitator script

1. Use the teaching guide in the kit. Do not open the rulebook
   during teaching.
2. Time the explanation from the first sentence to the start of
   play.
3. During play: answer rules questions, note each one, do not coach
   strategy. Note the game time and, twice per game, the wall-clock
   time of one full round.
4. After the game: each player fills the feedback form alone; the
   facilitator fills the observation fields.
5. Ask the goal check before feedback: each player explains, without
   help, how someone wins this game. Note where they hesitate.

## What to record (observations only)

- Minutes to explain the rules, against the session-001 number.
- Total play duration, and whether the endgame drags once a claim
  stands.
- Count and content of rules clarifications. Session 001's top two
  were the Prestige economy and duel-bonus bookkeeping; watch
  whether the one-modifier rule removes the recounting.
- The four understanding probes (ask each player, note who answered
  without help):
  1. What does your scutum do in a duel? What if you also hold a
     gladius?
  2. A rival's claim has stood for one full round. What happens on
     the second, and what can you still do?
  3. You hold no Prestige and a claim stands. Name every way you can
     get back into the game.
  4. Why might you walk through the middle of the board?
- The interior treasures: did anyone plan a route through (12,3) or
  (6,9) on purpose? Did the middle feel alive or still rim-bound?
- The interior guard posts: did farmers use them? Did anyone call a
  post unfair to reach?
- Claim window: how many claims survived, were canceled inside the
  window, or died to the fee (could not pay). Did the standing
  claimant move at all, and why?
- Duel shame: did the loser's payment change anyone's willingness to
  duel? Did anyone hit zero Prestige and stall?
- The six abilities: for each card, note if its holder used it, and
  whether any ability was never used across the session.
- The first completed set: did that player experience the claim as a
  prize or a burden? Simulation says they win about a third of
  games; session 001 read it as a burden.
- The board switch: was the move to the succession map clear? Did
  the return to gates read as a fresh start or as lost position?
- The meeting places: did any seat feel their nearest site was too
  far, or too near a rival's? (Design: every gate 3 to 5 steps from
  its nearest site.)
- Phase-2 income: with no guard posts on the succession map, did
  anyone run dry and stall? (Simulation shows the farmer profile
  weakening; duel wins are the only endgame income.)
- Alternative plays: ask each player for one turn where they wanted
  two different actions. Record the turn and both options.
- Would they play again, and what would they try differently.

## Known questions this round should answer

1. Does the two-round window feel tense, or like waiting? Does
   anyone lose track of how long the claim has stood?
2. Does the one-modifier duel rule end the mid-duel recounting that
   session 001 hit?
3. Do the six abilities read as six different reasons to explore, or
   as six lines of text to forget?
4. Does the crossflow middle earn its traffic in human play, or do
   players orbit the rim out of habit?
5. Is the contest fee understood as the price of intervention, or a
   hidden rule that surprises someone mid-challenge?
6. Does any seat feel disadvantaged on the succession map before
   the facilitator mentions the measurements? (The single board had
   a persistent east deficit; the twin board measures .266/.250/
   .242/.237.)
7. What one rule caused the most questions in every group?

## Scoring

Record answers only. Do not compute a numeric fun score. A session
counts as positive evidence only if players can state the goal, name
a real choice they made, and want another game.

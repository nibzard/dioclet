# Human playtest protocol (V0.8 succession)

Status: prepared, not yet run. No human session has been recorded.
Update this file with actual sessions only; keep predictions in a
separate notes column, never in the recorded fields.

This protocol tests the V0.8 succession prototype: persistent
equipment, Prestige, faction support, and the contested claim. It
replaces `protocol.md` (V0.6.1) for this ruleset. The V0.6.1 file
stays for the old Emperor-fight game.

## Materials

One printed `playtest/playtest-pack.html` (V0.8 board, equipment
cards, player trackers, rulebook, teaching script, feedback form),
two six-sided movement dice, one six-sided challenge die, counters
for Prestige, four pawns, a timer, and a pencil.

Record with each session: the date, the rules file and its hash, the
seed and gate assignment used, and the number of rounds.

## Recommended groups

Same groups as V0.6.1: one children's group with an adult
facilitator, one mixed-age family, four adult strategy players, and
two to four adults who do not play board games regularly. One
session per group is enough for the first round.

Play at least one four-game gate rotation with the adult strategy
group. Each player uses every gate once. Note any gate that feels
better or worse and why.

## Facilitator script

1. Use the five-minute teaching script in the pack. Do not open the
   rulebook during teaching.
2. Time the explanation from the first sentence to the start of play.
3. During play: answer rules questions, note each one, do not coach
   strategy. Note the game time and, twice per game, the wall-clock
   time of one full round.
4. After the game: each player fills the feedback form alone; the
   facilitator fills the observation fields.
5. Ask the goal check before feedback: each player explains, without
   help, how someone wins this game. Note where they hesitate.

## What to record (observations only)

- Minutes to explain the rules.
- Total play duration in minutes.
- Count and content of rules clarifications.
- The four understanding probes (ask each player, note who answered
  without help):
  1. Why does collecting equipment help you later?
  2. What is Prestige for? Name two ways to get it.
  3. How do you earn faction support?
  4. A rival just proclaimed a claim. What can you do about it?
- Prestige: did players spend it on support, on a boosted challenge,
  or hoard it? Did anyone sit at the cap and waste rewards?
- Claims: how many claims were proclaimed, challenged, canceled, and
  crowned per game. Did the response window feel tense or empty?
- Waiting: turns where a player had no interesting choice. When and
  why.
- Alternative plays: ask each player for one turn where they wanted
  two different actions. Record the turn and both options.
- Would they play again, and what would they try differently.

## Known questions this round should answer

1. Do players understand that equipment persists into succession
   without free grants?
2. Is one Prestige for one support understood without a second
   explanation?
3. Does the contested claim produce a dramatic finish, or repeated
   cancellation and frustration?
4. Does the contest fee (if the tested candidate uses one) read as a
   strategic cost or as a hidden rule?
5. Do players ever choose an ordinary duel over other actions on
   purpose? What makes them do it?
6. Does any gate feel stronger or weaker across the rotation, and do
   players name the same one?
7. What one rule caused the most questions in every group?

## Scoring

Record answers only. Do not compute a numeric fun score. A session
counts as positive evidence only if players can state the goal, name
a real choice they made, and want another game.

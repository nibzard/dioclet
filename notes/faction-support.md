# Faction support and imperial succession

Research date: 2026-11-08.

Design context: [Teachable facts](../docs/teachable-facts.md), [treasure incentives](treasure-incentives.md), and [current browser rules](../web/rules.js).

## User direction

The user finds seals complicated and weakly motivated. They propose replacing seals with faction support or support from competing players. The game should draw more meaning from the Tetrarchy and the struggle for imperial power.

The useful principle from the existing history note is to teach through play. Changing the label on the same three unrelated tests would not fully address this request.

## Verified history

- The Tetrarchy included two senior emperors, the Augusti, and two junior emperors, the Caesars. Junior emperors were intended to succeed their senior partners.
- The succession system faced challenges after the joint abdication in 305.
- Constantine was proclaimed emperor by his troops in York after his father's death in 306. Military backing therefore provides a concrete historical connection for the game.

The game can draw inspiration from contested succession after Diocletian's retirement. A contest between four claimants inside this palace is fictional. The proposed three factions and their endorsement rules are also game abstractions, not a documented Roman election procedure.

## Recommended prototype

Replace the two seals with support from any two of three groups:

| Game faction | Meaning in play | Suggested meeting place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Army | Troops back your claim | Barracks |
| Imperial officials | Administrators recognize your authority | Peristyle |
| City elites | Local wealth and connections back you | A city district or market site |

Use one rule for every group: visit its meeting place and spend one Glory to gain its support. Keep one action per turn. There is no separate faction-specific die test. Glory now represents prestige that can be converted into political backing. The price of one Glory is a starting test value, not an established balance result.

For the first prototype, support remains with a player once earned and is available to every player. This provides a simple replacement for the existing prerequisite. Exclusive banners, shifting loyalties, and bidding are separate experiments. They should not all be introduced with the basic change.

The intended endgame becomes a succession contest between claimants. The existing two attacks against Diocletian need a separate decision; renaming them does not by itself make that contest political.

## Support from other players

Requiring endorsements from rivals would add negotiation, but it also permits refusal to block the game and allows a losing player to choose the winner. Start with independent factions. If player endorsements are later introduced, they should be optional and have explicit limits and consequences.

## Connections to the earlier concerns

- Support gives Glory a clear purpose beyond combat bonuses.
- Earning support during the city phase could provide a useful spending option before transition. That is a further rule change to test.
- This proposal does not establish the best Glory cap or solve the free-equipment incentive problem by itself.
- Keeping three distinct roll tests under faction names would retain much of the complexity the user objects to.

## Open decisions

1. Is support available only in the palace phase, or can players build it during city preparation?
2. Which board sites represent the groups without implying invented historical room functions?
3. Does the final contest use rival challenges, a contested proclamation, or another simple resolution?
4. Should support remain permanent, or can rivals contest it in a later version?

This is a design proposal. No balance measurements are claimed for it.

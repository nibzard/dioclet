# Narrative playtest 6 — corrected retreat rule

- Run: `runs/narr6`, seed 4201, rules baseline, engine 0.1.0.
- Outcome (from `runs/narr6/result.json`): P2 "Vela" wins by Emperor,
  turn 57 (36 in level 1, 21 in level 2), 15 rounds, no phase timeout.
- Final line before the win: P0, P2, P3 all sat at 1 hit; Vela rolled
  6 against the Emperor's 3 and took the crown on her third attempt.

## How the corrected endgame felt

- The retreat penalty stings for real now. Vela's first Emperor attack
  (turn 49, Glory spent) tied 8-8 and dumped her from the chamber door
  to [2,3]; she lost her last Glory plus a full turn of tempo and only
  re-attacked three turns later. That miss plausibly cost her the
  window, and she still won because everyone else missed too.
- Ties hurt more than low rolls. Two of the three misses in round 4
  were ties (8-8 and 5-5) that the Emperor keeps, so a paid +2 attack
  still fails on a 6-vs-4 roll. Players read this as harsh but fair.
- Chamber camping is slower. Every miss recycles you through [2,3],
  so nobody could chain attacks on consecutive turns unless already
  parked on the retreat square. Stola, already sitting at [2,3], was
  the only one whose miss cost no extra movement — camping the retreat
  square is now a real tactic, which feels odd but adds a choice.
- The two-hit race stayed tense to the last die: three players at 1
  hit within five turns of each other, and the leader changed twice
  (P2, then P3/P0 catching up) before Vela closed it out.
- Paid attacks still dominate the opener but not the close. All four
  players entered level 2 with 6 equipment, so the +2 Glory attack
  (about 72 percent) ruled the first attempt; after Glory ran dry the
  free attack (about 42 percent) decided the game. Two free hits fell
  in the final three turns.
- The peristyle Glory purchase is an instant-seal button and everyone
  who could pressed it (P2, P0, P3). It compressed the seal phase to
  about two rounds; only Hasta, who refuses to pay, lagged at one seal
  and never reached the Emperor. Free-seal players finished the
  endgame with no Glory left for the fight itself.
- Boring stretches: the level 1 equipment tail (rounds 7-9) dragged —
  four players walking revealed districts for the sixth copy of each
  item with no interaction. Zero duels happened all game; nobody
  attacked a rival even when passing them.
- The phase transition reset everyone to their gates, wiping the level
  1 positioning race in one event. The two rounds of walking that
  followed felt like filler before seals opened.
- Verdict: the fix works. Misses now cost Glory, tempo, and position,
  which made the Emperor window feel earned rather than a free re-roll
  lottery.
